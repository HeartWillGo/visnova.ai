# AI Fashion Production Costs and ROI: A SKU Pilot That Counts the Whole Workflow

> Long-form blog · cost and ROI guide

![AI Fashion Production Costs and ROI: A SKU Pilot That Counts the Whole Workflow](./assets/illustration.png)

## Ask which work disappears—not just what one generation costs

A generation fee is only one line in the comparison between a conventional shoot and AI-assisted production. A photo shoot may include sample shipping, model and photographer time, studio, lighting, styling, retouching, travel, scheduling, and reshoots. An AI workflow adds source-image preparation, prompt iteration, failed outputs, manual repair, review, rights checks, subscriptions, storage, and editing. The comparison is meaningful only when both sides include the same deliverables and review standard.

## Build a full-cost model per SKU

Track for each SKU: shoot or tool fees, credit consumption per attempt, total attempts, usable-output rate, retouch minutes, review and editing time, asset-management effort, and elapsed days from preparation to delivery. A useful frame is: total cost per approved asset = direct fees + failed-generation costs + production hours × internal hourly rate + review/management time + finishing costs. Insert your own payroll, invoices, and contracts. A platform’s free allowance or advertised speed is not your verified unit cost.

Separate work that may be substituted from work that remains. AI might help change a background, add mild movement to an approved on-model photograph, or create channel crops. It may not replace product-construction approval, true fabric disclosure, fit testing, or elaborate styling. Compare photography savings with the added review and repair burden instead of reporting only the lower visible tool bill.

## Use a reproducible example, not a promise

Imagine a team creates launch images for 20 SKUs each week. Its conventional workflow uses 30 labor hours for photography and finishing. In an AI pilot, each SKU gets four generations, only two are usable, and the team spends another 18 hours reviewing and repairing them. Even with a smaller tool invoice, total labor may not fall. Savings become possible if the next run raises the usable rate and cuts correction time. These figures are illustrative only; they are not Visnova pricing, efficiency, or revenue claims.

A simple ROI formula is: (verified cost savings + measured incremental business contribution − added AI costs) ÷ added AI costs. If conversion impact has not been measured, do not count a sales lift yet. Report creation cost, review cost, reuse rate, and publishing outcomes separately so one vague ROI percentage does not hide a workflow problem.

## Design a pilot that ties output to labor

Choose one category, one aspect ratio, and one channel. Log the conventional baseline, source preparation, generation attempts, review and repair, and delivery time. Use comparable source-photo quality, the same garment checklist, and the same reviewers to reduce bias. Useful measures include cost per approved asset, usable assets per SKU, repair hours, time to approval, and cross-channel reuse. For product comparisons, give every platform the same garment reference, generate more than once, and keep failed versions rather than showing only the best sample.
