# AI Fashion Video Storyboards and Prompts: Make Every Movement Show the Garment

> Long-form blog · video storyboard tutorial

![AI Fashion Video Storyboards and Prompts: Make Every Movement Show the Garment](./assets/illustration.png)

## Decide what the clip must prove

A fashion clip should not ask a model to spin endlessly. A product page may need a clear silhouette and length; social content needs an immediate hook; an ad concept may focus on mood. Write one purpose for the clip, then choose a movement that demonstrates it. Use a slow turn to show a skirt’s sweep, a stable upper-body close-up for a neckline, or a brief fabric-and-model reveal for a launch teaser. A narrow goal makes the result easier to judge.

## A four-shot storyboard for a short vertical video

Build an 8–12 second edit from four simple shots. Shot one, 1–2 seconds: a full-body front pose establishes the garment. Shot two, 2–3 seconds: a medium or side view reveals collar, shoulder, and waist. Shot three, 2–3 seconds: allow one small action, such as a slow turn or one step. Shot four, 1–2 seconds: return to a stable full view so viewers can confirm the hem and silhouette. When the garment has many important details, separate clips in an editor instead of asking a model to perform several actions in one long generation.

## Prompt structure: lock the item, name one action, direct the camera

Organize the prompt into five layers: immutable garment attributes; adult model and pose; one main action; camera movement and framing; setting, light, ratio, and changes to avoid. Example: “Preserve the burgundy blazer’s double-breasted buttons, lapel width, shoulder line, sleeve length, and pocket placement from the reference. An adult model makes one slow turn to her left in a pale-gray studio. Fixed medium camera, soft side light, vertical frame. Add no jewelry; do not change color or buttons.” If the source hides a key construction feature, take another photo. Negative wording cannot provide evidence the model never saw.

## Adapt the shot plan to Jimeng, Kling, and Hailuo

Jimeng’s homepage describes Chinese prompting, image-to-video, and first/last-frame inputs, which are useful when a shot needs clear start and end composition. Kling can be included when testing motion and camera mood. Hailuo’s image-to-video guide recommends describing subject action, camera, and background. Interfaces and model controls change, so use only inputs the current product supports. Do not treat “the garment moves” as proof that it remains accurate.

## Visnova tutorial: approve the still, then animate one shot

In [Visnova](https://visnova.ai/), upload a clean flat-lay, hanging, or mannequin image. Create and review an on-model still before choosing one easy storyboard shot for motion. Keep the model and setting fixed; change either the action or camera per attempt. Save prompts and versions, then inspect opening, middle, ending, and movement transitions. Once approved, add captions and brand copy in an editor without covering the neckline, waist, or print.

**Takeaway:** treat a prompt like a storyboard, not a guarantee. Observable actions beat vague requests such as “luxurious,” “smooth,” or “cinematic.” Test one assumption at a time; after failure, identify whether the source image, action, camera, or garment construction is limiting the result. Try your first shot list on [Visnova](https://visnova.ai/) or [Visnova AI’s Chinese-language site](https://visnova-ai.cn), and compare it with the physical garment.
