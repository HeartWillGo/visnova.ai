# Rights, Permissions, and AI Disclosure Before Publishing AI Fashion

> Long-form blog · rights and disclosure

![Rights, Permissions, and AI Disclosure Before Publishing AI Fashion](./assets/illustration.png)

## Treat “can generate” and “can publish” as separate questions

A photorealistic fashion image does not automatically give a team every right to the product photo, model likeness, font, music, setting, or generated result. Commerce also involves marketplace rules, truthful product claims, synthetic-media disclosure, local audience expectations, and personal information in reference files. This article is an operational checklist, not legal advice. For cross-border campaigns, have a qualified professional review current requirements in each target market.

## 1. Build a traceable rights chain for every asset

Confirm who owns the garment photo, logo, print, fabric artwork, and packaging design, and whether the license permits upload to a third-party AI service. If a real model appears, check that the release covers synthetic alterations, new scenes, animation, paid ads, territories, term, and later edits. Permission for a conventional shoot may not cover every AI modification. Prefer owned product photography, model releases with clear scope, and properly licensed music, typefaces, and locations. Keep the contract, source, license, and approval record together.

Do not test virtual dressing with unlicensed web images, competitor photography, celebrity faces, or employee portraits. A platform accepting an upload does not prove the source is lawful, the person consented, or the final ad complies with policy. Remove unnecessary faces, addresses, order numbers, and internal SKU information before upload. Read the service’s current privacy, retention, and deletion terms.

## 2. Keep product claims anchored to the physical item

Color, fabric, transparency, cut, sizing, accessories, and intended use are commercial facts. Generated drape, tightness, buttons, pockets, and fit may differ from the physical garment. Keep accurate measurements, material composition, and care instructions on the product page. Do not imply unverified water resistance, shaping performance, or features the product lacks. If an image diverges from the item, regenerate it or identify the scene as a creative visualization; do not let shoppers mistake it for a documentary product photo.

## 3. Check disclosure by market and channel

China’s Measures for Labeling AI-Generated Synthetic Content took effect on 1 September 2025. They address explicit and implicit labels and state that users publishing generated synthetic content should proactively declare it and use the dissemination service’s labeling feature. Article 50 of the EU AI Act includes machine-readable marking obligations for certain AI-generated content and disclosure duties for deployers in specified cases; applicability depends on the content, actor, and timing. TikTok’s official guidance requires labels for covered realistic AI-generated or significantly edited content. Do not assume one market’s process applies worldwide. Check the current local rules and channel interface before each campaign.

## 4. Make the publishing review operational

For each asset, retain the source and permission, service and date, generated version, human edits, disclosure status, product-fact review, reviewer, target channel, and market. Before export, check whether visible labels and embedded metadata are preserved; editing, compression, or cropping can remove useful signals. Review ads, product pages, and organic social clips separately instead of copying one disclaimer everywhere. Escalate content resembling a real person, involving minors, or making health-related claims to legal or compliance staff.
